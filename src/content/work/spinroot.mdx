Spinroot pairs a quiet morning farm with a torchlit underground cave, joined by one shared mechanic: the Wishing Reel. Every dawn you turn a 3x3 folklore shrine with clover tokens to summon helper critters, seasonal seed packets, and the day's weather. Bad luck costs time or a weed-clearing chore, never progress, and real money never touches the shrine.

## Bending the odds instead of chasing randomness

The Wishing Reel runs an 8-line paytable (three rows, three columns, two diagonals) across eight symbols: Mole, Bee, Newt, Seed, Sun, Rain, wild Clover, and Weeds. An odds panel displays every symbol's current weight openly. Feeding helpers raises tomorrow's Luck, charms bias individual symbols, Hold locks a column for a spin, and a dry-spin pity rule guarantees a helpful line after four blank turns.

Spins resolve from the day's seed and spin counter so reloading cannot spoof outcomes. I wrote two headless simulation suites (`sim_reel.gd` over 100,000 spins and `sim_season.gd` over full 56-day years) to verify that the shrine adds flavor and strategy without overshadowing actual farming income.

## Helpers, two seasons, and strict pixel discipline

Each summoned critter carries a surface job and a dungeon role: Moles clear weeds for compost and tank underground grubs, Bees pollinate crops for gold-quality harvests and sting at range, and Newts water dry soil and heal the hero in combat. Three matching helpers merge into a stronger tier, or train up in the Den.

Built in Godot 4.6 (GL Compatibility) at 640x360 integer-scaled resolution with typed GDScript, every gameplay state lives in plain `RefCounted` objects owned by one `World` model while nodes only render. The vertical slice passes 148 headless automated tests plus live window checks for 144Hz camera pacing, banded night lighting, and zero sub-pixel shimmer.
